Price vs Value: Why the Lowest RFQ Can Cost More

By Avenir SolutionsApril 10, 20262 min read

In procurement, one of the most common mistakes is selecting a supplier based solely on the lowest Request for Quote (RFQ) price. While it seems logical to choose the cheapest option, this approach often leads to significantly higher total costs and operational challenges.

The Hidden Costs of Cheap Suppliers

When you select a supplier purely on price, you may be overlooking numerous hidden costs that will impact your bottom line:

Quality Issues and Rework

Lower-cost suppliers often cut corners on quality control. This results in higher defect rates, requiring inspection, rework, and scrap. A single batch of defective parts can cost far more to address than the savings from the lower unit price.

Supply Chain Disruptions

Unreliable suppliers cause delays, missed deadlines, and production interruptions. The cost of stopping a production line, expediting alternative suppliers, or disappointing customers far exceeds any savings from a lower RFQ.

Lack of Technical Support

Cheap suppliers often provide minimal technical support. When issues arise, you're left to solve problems alone, consuming internal resources and time.

Inconsistent Delivery

Low-cost suppliers may have inconsistent on-time delivery rates. This forces you to carry higher inventory levels to buffer against delays, tying up working capital.

Total Cost of Ownership Framework

Instead of comparing unit prices, procurement professionals should evaluate Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), which includes:

  • Unit Price: The quoted price per unit
  • Quality Costs: Inspection, rework, warranty claims, and scrap
  • Delivery Costs: Expediting, inventory carrying costs, and supply chain disruptions
  • Support Costs: Technical assistance, problem resolution, and relationship management
  • Risk Costs: Supplier financial stability, geopolitical factors, and market volatility

Real-World Example

Consider a manufacturing company that selected a supplier based on a 15% lower RFQ price. Within six months, they experienced:

  • 8% defect rate (vs. 0.5% from their previous supplier)
  • 40% late deliveries
  • Significant rework costs
  • Production delays costing $50,000 per incident

The "savings" from the lower price were quickly consumed by quality issues and operational disruptions. The total cost of ownership was actually 30% higher than their previous supplier.

Evaluating Supplier Value

When evaluating RFQs, consider:

  • Quality Metrics: Ask for defect rates, certifications, and quality management systems
  • Delivery Performance: Request on-time delivery percentages and lead time consistency
  • Technical Capability: Assess their ability to support your needs and solve problems
  • Financial Stability: Evaluate their ability to sustain operations and invest in improvements
  • Relationship Approach: Look for suppliers interested in partnership, not just transactions
  • References: Contact existing customers about their experience

Building Value-Based Supplier Relationships

The best procurement strategy focuses on value, not just price. This means:

  • Developing long-term relationships with reliable suppliers
  • Collaborating on continuous improvement
  • Sharing forecasts to enable better planning
  • Recognizing and rewarding supplier performance
  • Building partnerships based on mutual success
